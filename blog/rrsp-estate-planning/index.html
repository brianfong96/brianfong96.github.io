The RRSP estate tax trade-off

Estate planningInteractive guide

Should you withdraw retirement savings gradually, leave them invested for longer, or buy life insurance to protect your children's inheritance? The answer depends on tax rates, investment returns, longevity, insurance costs and whether you can afford to give the money away.

1. What the original infographic proposes

Linda's situation

Age 67, $650,000 in an RRSP, two adult sons. The proposed gift is approximately $583 per month per son, or $14,000 total per year, to their TFSAs.

Valid principle: cash gifts generally do not incur a Canadian gift tax. RRSP withdrawals are taxable to Linda. A large remaining RRSP is normally included in her terminal income unless an eligible rollover applies.

Missing details: the gross withdrawal must exceed $14,000 to cover tax; the 53.53% Ontario rate is marginal, not a flat charge; the children need contribution room; and Linda may need the assets for her own care.

3. What actually makes a strategy win?

Withdraw early or compound longer?

Both an RRSP and a TFSA shelter investment growth. If the return and eventual tax rate are identical, paying tax earlier does not inherently improve the result. Early withdrawals can win when they convert highly taxed future income into lower-tax income today. Holding can win when assets outside the RRSP would face investment tax, or when early withdrawals push you into higher brackets.

Does aggressive investing plus insurance solve the problem?

Try setting the holding return to 8% and the withdrawal return to 3%, then repeat with both at 8%. The first test mixes asset allocation with withdrawal timing; the second isolates timing. The same equities can generally be held in either a TFSA or RRSP. Insurance pays only on a covered death, not on an investment loss. A market crash can still leave a retiree unable to cover care or premiums. Try the first-year decline stress test.

Term or permanent?

Term is a temporary hedge for early death. Once coverage ends the premiums are gone and no benefit is payable. Permanent coverage may offset tax whenever death occurs if maintained, but high premiums consume funds that could otherwise compound. A fixed benefit may not cover an aggressively growing RRSP. A policy can lapse if the owner runs out of money; the simulator flags that rather than assuming free coverage.

Keep control of retirement money

Gifts are irreversible. Protect retirement spending and long-term care first. The model leaves the initial cash reserve with Linda, treats gifts as children's assets, and never uses their assets to pay her bills. An actual plan could retain more cash or use Linda's own TFSA instead. Named beneficiaries may help with probate but do not erase RRSP income tax.

4. How the numbers work

Each year: grow assets, calculate planned or required RRIF withdrawal, pay incremental progressive income tax and OAS recovery, cover retirement shortfalls and premiums, then allocate surplus gifts to available TFSA room and taxable investments. At death, include the remaining registered balance in the same year's income and add any active policy benefit. The model starts mandatory RRIF withdrawals at age 72, assuming conversion at 71. Premiums are paid from cash or extra registered withdrawals where needed; no premiums after term expiry.

2026 tax bands and selected basic credits are held constant for comparison. Ontario surtax and health premium and an approximate OAS recovery are modeled; other credits, benefit eligibility, provincial details and tax law changes are not fully modeled. The taxable-account return is an assumed net return, including a simplified allowance for investment taxation; there is no separate terminal capital gains calculation. All returns are nominal, smooth and deterministic except the optional first-year stress. Real-dollar results use your inflation assumption. No probability of death, individualized underwriting, policy cash value, dividends, renewal, surrender charges, probate or spouse rollover is modeled. Life insurance is not an investment guarantee. This is educational, not a personalized recommendation.
