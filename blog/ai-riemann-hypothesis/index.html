How AI helped prove the Riemann Hypothesis

It didn't count every prime. It helped find a mathematical construction that can grow with the problem, and an error bound precise enough to rule out an entire class of counterexamples.

I want to understand what AI actually discovered, not just how many lines of Lean it generated. This guide approaches the same argument at three depths. Start with the intuition, then follow the formulas, then open the technical dependencies and original proof.

Target exponent½

Square-root-scale fluctuations. The exact statistic and logarithmic factors matter; this is not a claim about the length of every prime gap.

Evidence status (Nov 26, 2026): Alpöge announced a Lean-formalized RH and Dirichlet GRH proof on Nov 20. Independent kernel checks are reported as successful; a much smaller edition also passes independent checks. A complete conventional exposition is still being developed. See verification and sources. This page is a study guide, not a new proof.

01 / ELI502 / University03 / Technical

Layer 01 / Intuition

Primes are trees on an infinite road

Put a tree at every prime: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13. Sometimes several are close together and sometimes there is empty road. We know primes become less dense, roughly one in every log(x) integers near x. RH is about how far their cumulative distribution can stray from a more precise prediction.

What would a counterexample do?

Imagine a hidden wave that causes the trees to become much too numerous or too sparse at sufficiently large scales. The real part of a zeta zero controls how quickly the size of such a wave grows. A zero off the critical line can create fluctuations that eventually outgrow the allowed scale.

Layer 01 / The breakthrough

Build a mathematical machine that scales

Think of trying to measure the trees indirectly. You can build a machine whose size tells you something about the primes it encodes. One machine is too imprecise. Connect many, and the useful information accumulates while the setup cost is spread across the chain.

Start lattice setup cost paid once
↓
Theta lift 1 collect divisibility information
↓
Theta lift 2 … m repeat the construction
↓
Final lattice compare size and divisibility

More lifts are not always better: each also incurs overhead. The remarkable observation, credited to Claude in the announcement (p. 1), is to make the chain length proportional to the square root of the range being studied.

This is a parameterized construction. The proof shows it works at the required arbitrarily large scales, rather than executing a different formula for every prime.

Layer 02 / Undergraduate mathematics

Why a problem about zeros is really about primes

Let s = σ + it. The zeta function is first defined in the half-plane σ > 1, where the series and Euler product converge absolutely:

ζ(s) = ∑n≥1 n−s = ∏p prime (1 − p−s)−1

The product follows from unique prime factorization. Analytic continuation defines ζ elsewhere (except its pole at 1). RH states that every nontrivial zero has real part ½; symmetry maps zeros on one side of the line to the other. The product alone cannot rule them out inside the critical strip.

From a count to a weighted error

Use Λ(n) = log p for prime powers n = ph, and 0 otherwise. Then ψ(x) = ∑n≤x Λ(n) is a weighted count. The prime number theorem says ψ(x) ∼ x. RH is equivalent to ψ(x) = x + O(√x log²x). The proof uses a more tractable smoothed error:

S(x) = ∑n≤x Λ(n)(x/n − 1)
G(x) = S(x) − x log x + (1 + γ)x

Here γ is Euler's constant. Small prime powers have larger weights, and terms go to zero continuously at the cutoff. The subtraction removes the expected main term.

How zeros create waves

G(x) = log(2π) − ∑ρ xρ / [ρ(ρ − 1)] + O(x−2)

For ρ = β + it, xρ = xβ eit log x. The imaginary part determines frequency on the log scale; β determines amplitude growth. Since different waves can cancel, the rigorous conclusion uses a Landau-type oscillation criterion, not just a comparison of one term.

Derive the connection using the Mellin transform

For Re(s) > 1, logarithmically differentiate the Euler product to obtain −ζ′(s)/ζ(s) = ∑ Λ(n)n−s. Integrate the ramp against x−s−1 to get n−s/[s(s−1)].

∫1∞ G(x)x−s−1 dx
= −ζ′(s)/[ζ(s)s(s−1)] − 1/(s−1)² + (1+γ)/(s−1)

The last two terms cancel the principal part at s = 1; nontrivial zeros produce poles with residues weighted by multiplicity. Shifting the inverse Mellin contour gives the explicit formula. See Rubinstein, §I.1 and Part II.

Layer 02 / Building the machine

Divisibility is a measuring instrument

If a nonzero integer N is divisible by peₚ for different primes, their product is at most |N|. Taking logarithms gives ∑ eₚ log p ≤ log|N|. That elementary inequality converts size into a constraint on primes.

Ordinary factorials lose precision because the exponent of p contains floor functions. The construction replaces integers by maps between lattices (discrete modules with metrics), where local divisibility and global size can be compared. It uses arithmetic-geometric objects with smoother valuation bounds.

What is a theta lift?

A theta series encodes lattice points according to a quadratic form. For a square lattice it is ∑a,b∈ℤ qa²+b². The coefficient of qr counts representations of r as a sum of two squares. A theta lift uses a richer theta kernel to transfer a function between automorphic spaces.

(Tf)(h) = ∫ Θ(g,h) f(g) dg

Think of a kernel as a structured transformation: in the definite finite setting it can be viewed as a weighted matrix acting on function values. In the general setting this schematic formula requires the appropriate conjugation, groups, measures and representation-theoretic conventions.

The chain repeats this transfer across automorphic spaces. The point is not that repetition magically eliminates error: the proof needs a divisibility gain at each step plus strong estimates for the norms, denominators and rank of the maps.

Layer 03 / Technical proof reader

The main inequality and the cost of every step

For the zeta specialization, the reconstruction's inequality (M) is stated using a table of residue classes. Auxiliary primes q and d determine characters χq, χd; n is the number of columns in a quotient of the units modulo a power of 2. Translates u in H move selected columns, t in T shifts the labels, and j indexes the m lifts.

Let Pu,t,j denote the weighted prime-power sum and Mu,t,j its expected main term. In each cell, positive weights w transform the local ramp via (a/ph − w)+ = w(a/(wph) − 1)+. This is how valuation estimates connect to the smoothed statistic.

(1/n) ∑u∈H ∑t∈T ∑j=1m (Pu,t,j − Mu,t,j) ≤ ℰ

ℰ/B ≤ xCε ( x/m + xm/k + m + k/n² )

B = |T| m b δ/2, where δ is the fraction of distinguished columns, b is the principal weight scale, k is a rank parameter, and C is an absolute constant. Uniformity in scale and admissibility of all parameters are part of the theorem; the bound is not available for arbitrary numeric inputs.

Derive the half exponent

At leading order take m ≍ x1/2, k ≍ x9/2, n ≍ x², as in §I.8 of the reconstruction. Then x/m = x1/2, xm/k = x−3, m = x1/2, and k/n² = x1/2. Thus the bracket is O(√x); the small slack in the actual parameters and xCε is handled by choosing ε appropriately.

Why worse step costs would break the result

If the normalized repeated cost were mr, balancing x/m and mr gives α = 1/(1+r), with dominant exponent r/(1+r). For r = 2, the result would only reach 2/3, even assuming the other estimates remained sufficient. This is a counterfactual algebra exercise, not an alternative proven theorem.

From inequality (M) to RH

Arithmetic input admissible parameters and the main inequality (M)
↓
Analytic input Dirichlet character estimates B and C; distinguish scales and average translates
↓
Smoothed bound G(x) ≪ε x½+ε on [Z, Z3/2] for arbitrarily large Z
↓
Oscillation criterion exclude nontrivial zeros with Re(ρ) > ½; apply functional-equation symmetry

The interval quantifier is important: a bound at isolated values of x is insufficient. Part II of the reconstruction establishes the analytic implication from (M) and the character estimates. Its Part III addresses the arithmetic side conditionally. The same formal development includes Dirichlet GRH; this exposition focuses on ζ.

Layer 03 / Dependency audit

What has to be proved for that machine to exist?

Balancing four terms is easy. Establishing (M) with those costs is the hard mathematics. Open each component below to see its role and the corresponding section of the human-readable reconstruction. The source's status table matters: none of its H0–H12 assumptions is proved outright in the draft, although several are reduced to named formal results. These are not extra axioms claimed for the verified Lean theorem.

01 · Local arithmetic: make divisibility accumulate

CM elliptic curves and height-two formal groups supply valuation estimates at inert primes. The ramp weights produce the desired prime-power statistic. Glue compatible local lattices, manage integrality and quantify gains/losses (H6, H7). Part III, §§4–6; §III.12: worked lemniscate example.

Toy local bound: vp(Aa) ≥ ⌊ap/(p²−1)⌋

For the lemniscate case at inert primes, this is a concrete smooth lower bound. It is not a general valuation formula for every theta kernel.

02 · Global geometry: control the size

Norms of the maps and covolumes of the initial and terminal lattices must match the main term through its linear order. The comparison uses Gaussian estimates, period formulas, inner-product formulas and mass/dimension formulas (H1, H4, H5). See §I.7 and Part III, §§4, 7.

03 · Denominators: stop the overhead from exploding

Changing local integral models and twists creates denominators. The comparison with Galois cohomology and bounds on those groups control these losses, entering the height and slope estimates (H1, H2, H9). Without that control the normalized repeated cost need not be linear in m. See §I.0, item 5 and Part III, §9.

04 · Rank and slopes: cover the target lattice

A single small-rank image cannot control the mean slope of a much larger lattice. Families of maps indexed by words need sufficiently spread images; trace-formula estimates and subspace/rank conditions lead to a mean-slope comparison. H8–H11 record these constraints. See Part III, §8 and §III.11.

05 · Assembly: cancel the main terms and bound the remainder

Compare local gains against global sizes and use common normalizations so the main terms cancel. Rubinstein's Theorem M assembles H0–H11 at one scale, and Corollary M9 averages translates. H12 gives admissible data uniformly along long intervals. Corollary M11 then combines the analytic results to obtain RH. See §III.11.

Research process / What AI did

What did the AI actually discover?

There is a difference between choosing a theorem, proposing a construction, proving its properties, and independently checking the resulting proof. Alpöge's account describes a human-guided search that moved through mathematical analogies before the theta-lift strategy emerged.

  1. Human frames the problem

    Alpöge brought a Stepanov-inspired analogy: use multiplicities to strengthen bounds, but ordinary integer arithmetic has no direct version of the relevant differentiation trick.

  2. AI proposes a scalable construction

    The idea is to simulate multiplicity by composing theta lifts. The choice of roughly √X lifts is explicitly credited to Claude. This is structural reasoning, not interpolation over known zeros.

  3. Work out the contracts

    Prove valuation, norm, denominator, rank and uniformity bounds. The short optimization only becomes meaningful once the hard mathematical dependencies have been established.

  4. Formalize and check

    Lean elaborates tactics into proof terms and its kernel checks them against precisely stated theorems. Other kernels can check exported terms. This validates deductions, not an AI's narrative about them.

  5. Compress and understand

    The shorter edition makes the dependencies easier to explore. Human-readable exposition, explanatory novelty and institutional acceptance are separate questions from formal validity.

For software engineers, this is closer to finding a new scalable algorithm with a formally specified contract than to asking a model to produce a plausible explanation. The difficult part is establishing the invariants. The proof checker provides a much stronger feedback signal than a language model's own confidence. The actual search prompts, failed branches, compute cost and per-lemma human/AI contributions are not fully documented by these sources, so we cannot infer the model's internal reasoning from the final artifact.

Discovery, verification and understanding are distinct stages. The proof is a compelling example of how a search process paired with rigorous verification can produce something worth studying.
